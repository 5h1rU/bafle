# Bafle

A music player whose music provider sits behind an Effect service, so the provider (Deezer today) can be swapped without touching the model or the app.

## Stack

- **Model**: `@alchemy.run/domain` (domain-driven design on Effect) in `packages/domain`. It imports only `@alchemy.run/domain` and `effect`.
- **Server**: a Cloudflare Worker in `packages/server` that hosts the model and holds the provider adapters.
- **App**: Foldkit (The Elm Architecture on Effect) in `packages/web`.
- **Infrastructure**: Alchemy, one stack in `alchemy.run.ts`, deployed to Cloudflare.

## Skills to load before working

- `alchemy-domain` for anything in `packages/domain` or `packages/server`. The copy in `.claude/skills/` comes from the installed `@alchemy.run/domain` package, so it matches the APIs the code compiles against.
- `foldkit-skills:foldkit` for anything in `packages/web`, with `packages/web/FOLDKIT.md` as the conventions for the installed Foldkit release.

Effect v4, Foldkit and Alchemy v2 are new and change often. Read the installed types in `node_modules` and the skills' references instead of writing their APIs from memory.

## Commands

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm test          # every package's tests
pnpm typecheck     # tsc -b over the whole monorepo
pnpm dev           # alchemy dev: Workers in local workerd, the app on Vite
```

`pnpm deploy` publishes to the Cloudflare account of the active Alchemy profile. Ask before running it.

## Where the provider is chosen

`packages/domain/src/Catalog/MusicCatalog.external.ts` declares what Bafle asks of a catalog, in its own words. `packages/server/src/Deezer/` is the only code that knows Deezer. `packages/server/src/BafleWorker.ts` names the adapter in use: to change provider, write another `MusicCatalog.toLayer(...)` and name it there.

## Navigation rules the app keeps

The app exists to show loading done well, so these are requirements, not style. Its pages are the three sections (Home `/`, New `/new`, Search `/search`), a track's page (`/track/:id`), an album's page (`/album/:id`), an artist's page (`/artist/:id`) and a playlist's page (`/playlist/:id`), all in `packages/web/src/route.ts`. Every tile on a shelf leads to the page of what it shows:

1. Going to a page whose data is not loaded holds the current page and marks what was chosen (the navigation item, the control at the end of the row, or the tile on a shelf: an album's, an artist's, a playlist's) as loading; the page swaps when the data arrives. One destination is never waited for: the page of the track the player holds. It is the player expanded, drawn from what the queue already knows, so it takes the screen and its history entry at once, and what the catalog adds fills in place.
2. The URL and the history move only when the screen does. A tap is an intention and lives in the Model; a history entry is a visit and is created when the destination takes the screen. Any number of taps that never get a reply leave history as it was. The exception is forced by the browser: on a direct link, a reload, Back or Forward the URL has already moved, so only the screen is held.
3. The latest intention wins, without a trace; replies to abandoned requests are dropped. A request with no answer after 10 seconds is a failure.
4. A page already loaded swaps in at once and refreshes in the background.
5. The first paint shows placeholders in the shape of the page, never a blank area.
6. A failure is said where the listener is. A track whose page fails to open from a list says so on its row, with "Try again", and the list stays as it is; so does an album asked from a row's menu. In the expanded player, a detail that fails to load says so under the controls, with "Try again", and the controls keep working. A page reached by its URL, and a section, say it on the page. So does an album chosen from a shelf: a tile has no more room to state a failure than a navigation item has, so the album's page takes the screen and says it, with "Try again" and the way back. An artist and a playlist are only ever chosen from a shelf, so theirs always does.
7. A failure that will not go away (a track, an album, an artist or a playlist the catalog does not have) says so without "Try again": on its row, whose menu stops offering the page, or on its page with the way back. An album can be on many rows: the row it was asked from says so, and every row on that album stops offering it. An artist and a playlist have no row, so each says it on its page.
8. Search keeps its previous results while a new term loads. Typing is the one navigation that is URL first, replacing its own entry, because the field's text lives in the URL. What each search found is kept under its term (`searched` in the Model), so a term searched for before shows its results at once, typed again or gone back to, and is asked for again behind them: after the typing pause, as any term is.
9. Going back to a list restores it exactly: same scroll position, same pictures already painted, nothing waiting on the network. Section pages stay mounted and hidden instead of being rebuilt, because Foldkit replaces the DOM when the view function at a position changes, and a rebuilt `<img>` repaints even when cached. An album's page is a list too, so it stays mounted behind a page reached from it (a song opened from one of its rows): one album at a time, the one on screen or else the nearest one behind it (`maybeAlbumIdInDocument` in `model.ts`). So are an artist's page, with their top songs and a shelf of their albums, and a playlist's, each kept the same way behind the album or the song reached from it (`maybeArtistIdInDocument`, `maybePlaylistIdInDocument`). Search is one page for every term: going back to an earlier search finds its results in the Model, with nothing waiting on the network, and the row the listener left it by is brought into view by the focus it is given; the scroll position itself is kept only for the search that was last on the page. Back to a list gives the focus to what the listener left it by: the row, or the tile of the album, the artist or the playlist.
10. A picture never appears as an empty box when a rendition of it is already loaded: show that one first and let the larger one replace it.
11. On a narrow screen the navigation floats over the foot of the page: a menu, and Search apart from it at the far end. A page scrolled down draws the menu back to a circle that shows where the listener is, and the island comes down into the row between the circle and Search. The top of the page brings the menu out again, and so does a press of the circle, where the page stands. A press made while the page is still running on from a flick keeps the menu out: that run was under way before the press and is no wish to send the menu away, so the following of the scroll waits for it to end (the page keeps still for a moment, or a finger is laid on it again) before it counts a way down. Choosing a section shows it whole; a track's page, an album's, an artist's and a playlist's leave it as it was, so closing the track puts everything back. Under the expanded player there is no navigation: closing it is the way out. It is drawn again, out of reach, only while the player's sheet is being swiped away and uncovers it. Drawn back, the circle is one control: the item it shows is a picture of where the listener is, taken out of the order of focus, and the control over it is the one thing there to press or to focus. How the navigation stands is in the Model (`navigation`). The scroll is not: the Subscription in `subscription.ts` tells only the two moments that change it. Drawn back, the circle and Search are smaller than the menu is tall and stand on its centre line, where its two ends were; the island keeps its height. Each piece has its own times, different on the way out and on the way back, read frame by frame off the app they follow: they are in `styles.css` under the `dock-` names. The navigation stands a fixed distance from the foot of the page and adds no safe-area inset to it.

## Playback rules the app keeps

The player is its own part of the app (`packages/web/src/player/`, a Foldkit Submodel) and lives only in the client: the domain does not know that anything is playing.

1. Tapping a row plays it, and the list it was in becomes the queue: on an album's page, the album; on an artist's, their top songs; on a playlist's, the playlist. A track's page opens from the menu at the end of its row; pressing the island expands it into the page of its track.
2. The island says only what the engine has said. A tap is a wish (`Acknowledged`, `Starting`); `Sounding` and `Waiting` come only from an engine report. Paused by the listener, stopped by the system and failed are three different states and look different.
3. Every order to the engine is numbered, and a report for an earlier order is dropped: the latest wish wins here too.
4. One audio element for the page's life. Navigation never touches it. Playing moves the route in one case only: an expanded player that goes on to another track rewrites its own history entry for that track, never adding one, so Back always closes it.
5. Sound starts inside the tap: `StartPlayback` calls `play()` as its first act, because phones refuse sound that does not come from a gesture.
6. Activity is shown only after 400 ms without sound, so a fast start never flashes a spinner.
7. A source that fails is asked of the catalog once more and retried from where it was. After that the island and the row say so, with a retry, and the queue never skips by itself.
8. The player knows no provider. `playableSource` in `player/model.ts` is the only reader of the catalog's audio field, and lengths and positions come from the engine, not from the catalog.
9. Expanded is derived, never stored. There is one route, `/track/:id`: the screen is the expanded player when the route's track is the player's current track, and the plain track page with a Play control and the island below otherwise. After a reload nothing is current, so a track's URL is its plain page.
10. Opening, the close control and the browser's Back are one view transition in two directions: the island and the expanded player share the name `player`, so the capsule grows into the screen and shrinks back, and the cover's name is on whichever of them is drawn. A track change inside the expanded player is no transition, and neither is a player swiped away: its sheet has already left the screen, and the page behind it is what the listener sees (`viewTransition` reads the sheet's `reach`). The island's own picture does not travel with the box that grows into the sheet: it is kept at the foot of that box, where the island stands, gone early on the way out and back late on the way in, so it is never seen to rise up the screen or to come down from a place it never stood. Both pictures carry the one name, so the app names the two transitions (`player-opens` and `player-closes`, from `viewTransition` in main.ts) and the style sheet knows by them which picture is the island's.

    The expanded player is drawn on a sheet (`packages/web/src/sheet/`, a Foldkit Submodel that follows React Aria's Sheet): the listener swipes it down to close it. The swipe is the browser's own scroll, not the app following a finger: the sheet stands on a stage inside a box that scrolls and snaps to the two places where the sheet is whole and where it is gone, so it has the momentum and the stretch of any scroll on the device (`sheet/geometry.ts`). What is in the sheet scrolls only while the sheet is whole, and from its top a swipe down goes on to the sheet. The Mount `FollowSheet` puts the box where the sheet is whole as it enters the document and tells the Model how much of the sheet is on screen (`reach`: `Whole`, `Partly`, `Gone`). The sheet also remembers a keyboard dismissal across the history callback. While the sheet is short of whole, the page the player was opened over is drawn behind it as it was left, with its navigation and its island, out of reach and with no names in a view transition, and a backdrop fades with the swipe by the sheet's own timeline (`sheet-backdrop` in styles.css). Swiped off the screen, the sheet says `Left` and the player closes as its control closes it, back through the history, with nothing more to see; with no page behind, Home takes the screen at once. Escape says `RequestedClose` and restores the previous page immediately, or Home on direct entry.

11. The island is one figure, light on the light page and dark on the dark one, with one outline round three things: the cover, a disc at its left end that stands out a little above and below the body; the body, which curves to meet it; and a swell at the other end that holds the terracotta control that starts and stops the sound. Past the swell a lower tail holds Next while there is room for it; in the row with the navigation's circle and Search the tail is drawn in and the swell is the island's end. Going there is three movements, each setting off after the one before so that each can be followed: Next travels left with the end of the tail and in under the main control, which stays where it is; the island narrows; and it comes down into the row. Narrowing and coming down are one movement on a spring, and so is everything else that moves there: each piece goes a little past its place and comes back to it, which is what makes the whole read as one soft movement. The springs were read off the app they follow, frame by frame, from where the island's main control stood in each: coming out, the island goes 4% past its place in a fifth of a second and is at rest in under half of one; drawing back, 2.5% past in three tenths. The menu and Search are stiffer, 1.5% and half of one percent, since a circle that overshot would be seen to lose its shape. They are the `--ease-island-` and `--ease-menu-` curves in styles.css, written out as `linear()` because CSS has no spring. Coming out is the same three the other way round. The figure takes the cover's colours near the cover, as bright as they are. The island's words are one colour throughout, dark on the light island and light on the dark one, whatever the cover. Its edge is its own colour: at rest there is no border to see, and a soft shadow tells it from the page. What travels that edge is a light, not a line: a short copper beam with an almost white head, and a soft glow round it that spills to both sides of the edge. Only the engine's word that there is sound sets it going, at a slow and steady pace. It never starts or stops at once: put to rest (a pause, a stop, a failure, sound that ran out of data) it slows to a halt where it is and dims to an ember that stays there, and set going again it brightens and gathers speed from that same place. A tap not yet answered changes nothing, however long the start takes, so going on to another track does not halt it and a first track does not light it. Previous and the progress bar are the expanded player's. What a control alone cannot explain (stopped by the system, failed) stands in a few words where the artist was, and is said whole to assistive technology. A title too long for the island is a marquee: it waits, travels across once with its copy following it in, and waits again. The island changing width under it disturbs nothing, since a pass goes as far as the title is long and not as its place is wide: the wait or the pass under way goes on, and a title that comes to fit in the middle of a pass ends that pass before it stands (`Finishing`). All of this is in the architecture, with nothing of its own outside it. What the light does (`light`) and where the title is in its waits and passes (`title`) are in the player's Model, the waits and the passes are Commands, and the view draws the figure, the light and the title from them. What only the document knows reaches it through Foldkit's `Mount`, an effect bound to one element (`player/mount.ts`): `MeasureTitle` tells the Model whether the title fits and how far a pass takes it; `DrawIslandFigure` writes the island's outline, worked out in `view/islandOutline.ts`, to the figure's paths in the very frame the island changes width, since a width sent through the Model would be drawn a frame late, and in that same frame puts the light back where the listener saw it, because the light is placed in shares of the outline and an outline that grows or shrinks would carry it along the edge; `StartIslandLight` sets the light's animations to what the Model says as the island enters the document. From then on `update` gives the Command `ChangeIslandLightPace` whenever the Model's `light` changes, and the light's pace changes little by little; a change that overtakes one under way carries on from the pace the light had reached. While a song changes, the island is one side of a view transition whose box is cut with `clip-path` drawn a little outside it, because the cover and the swell stand out of the island's box. The light and the shadow have view transition names of their own (`island-light` and `island-shadow`, in styles.css): a transition fades a still picture of the island into the live one, and a light inside that picture would stand still for as long as the fade lasts, as a shadow inside it would be cut where the picture is cut.

12. A row is one surface and ends in one control, three dots, that opens a menu of what else can be done with its track. A press on the row lights all of it, the control's place included, and a lit surface stands free: the line under its row and the line under the row above are not drawn, since a straight line would run into its rounded corners (`track-row` in styles.css). The menu offers, in this order: Play next, which puts the track right after the current one; Add to queue, which puts it after all the rest; Go to album, which is followed as a link to the page of the album the track is on would be; and Song details, which is followed as a link to the track's page would be (navigation rule 1). Go to album is offered on every row but those of the album's own page, for as long as the catalog has the album. A track is queued only once something is playing and is never the current track, the queue holds a track once among those to come, and queueing changes nothing that sounds. What was chosen is answered where it was chosen. Choosing a page, the song's or the album's, leaves the menu open: the item that was pressed turns its mark while the page is on its way, and if the page fails to open the item says so and a second press of it tries again, so nobody opens the menu twice to retry. The menu goes with the list when the page takes the screen. With the menu closed, the row's control turns instead, and the row says the failure under itself. Queueing closes the menu, and the control shows a tick for a moment, said in words to assistive technology only. What the catalog said of a track is kept under the track's id, so every row for that track reads it. An album is on many rows, so what the catalog said of it does not tell which row was used: the Model notes the row each album was last asked from (`albumRows`), and that row alone turns its control, or says under itself that the album could not be opened, with its own "Try again", or that it is not available. An album chosen from a shelf has no such row, and its tile turns instead. The menu is Foldkit UI's `Menu`, one Model for each row kept under the row's id (`rowMenus`; a menu never touched is not kept), and `rowMenu.ts` says what it can offer. It is not modal: its backdrop covers the screen and hears the press that closes it.

## How it looks and installs

1. The palette is aubergine, built on the two colours of the mark: the deep aubergine of the speaker (`#46203f`) and the pale orchid it stands on (`#efdfec`). Every colour is a token in `styles.css`, light and dark, and no class names a colour of its own.
2. A shelf snaps: a swipe along it comes to rest with a tile at its leading edge.
3. An album's cover travels between its tile on a shelf and the head of its page, as a track's does between its row and its page: the tile's picture and the page's carry one name on the page on screen (`albumCoverTransitionStyle`). An artist's picture and a playlist's travel the same way (`artistPictureTransitionStyle`, `playlistPictureTransitionStyle`).
4. Bafle installs to a home screen. The manifest, the icons and the mark are in `packages/web/public` (their sources in `design/`). While an iPhone starts the installed app it shows a launch picture of the screen's exact size, light or dark (`public/splash`, drawn from the mark); while the app itself loads, in any browser, the same mark stands in the page (`#splash` in `index.html`) and gives way when the app draws. There is no service worker: the app needs the network to be of any use, and nothing is kept for offline.

The engine is the `AudioEngine` service (`player/audioEngine.ts`); `packages/web/src/entry.ts` names the implementation in `resources`, and tests name a fake.
