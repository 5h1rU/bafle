# Bafle

A music player whose music provider sits behind an Effect service, so the provider (Deezer today) can be swapped without touching the model or the app.

## Stack

- **Model**: `@alchemy.run/domain` (domain-driven design on Effect) in `packages/domain`. It imports only `@alchemy.run/domain` and `effect`.
- **Server**: a Cloudflare Worker in `packages/server` that hosts the model and holds the provider adapters.
- **App**: Foldkit (The Elm Architecture on Effect) in `packages/web`.
- **Infrastructure**: Alchemy, one stack in `alchemy.run.ts`, deployed to Cloudflare.

## Skills to load before working

- `alchemy-domain` for anything in `packages/domain` or `packages/server`. The copy in `.claude/skills/` comes from the installed `@alchemy.run/domain` package, so it matches the APIs the code compiles against.
- `foldkit-skills:foldkit` for anything in `packages/web`, with `packages/web/FOLDKIT.md` as the conventions for the installed Foldkit release.

Effect v4, Foldkit and Alchemy v2 are new and change often. Read the installed types in `node_modules` and the skills' references instead of writing their APIs from memory.

## Commands

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm test          # every package's tests
pnpm typecheck     # tsc -b over the whole monorepo
pnpm dev           # alchemy dev: Workers in local workerd, the app on Vite
```

`pnpm deploy` publishes to the Cloudflare account of the active Alchemy profile. Ask before running it.

## Where the provider is chosen

`packages/domain/src/Catalog/MusicCatalog.external.ts` declares what Bafle asks of a catalog, in its own words. `packages/server/src/Deezer/` is the only code that knows Deezer. `packages/server/src/BafleWorker.ts` names the adapter in use: to change provider, write another `MusicCatalog.toLayer(...)` and name it there.

## Navigation rules the app keeps

The app exists to show loading done well, so these are requirements, not style. Its pages are the five sections (Home `/`, New `/new`, Genres `/genres`, Radio `/radio`, Search `/search`), a track's page (`/track/:id`), an album's page (`/album/:id`), an artist's page (`/artist/:id`), a playlist's page (`/playlist/:id`), a genre's page (`/genre/:id`) and a station's page (`/station/:id`), all in `packages/web/src/route.ts`. Genres shows every genre the catalog files music under, as tiles; a genre's page shows what is played most in it: its top songs, and shelves of albums, artists and playlists. Radio shows the catalog's stations on shelves, one for each genre that has any; a station's page lists the songs it plays now, as a playlist's lists its own. Every tile leads to the page of what it shows:

1. Going to a page whose data is not loaded holds the current page and marks what was chosen (the navigation item, the control at the end of the row, or the tile: an album's, an artist's, a playlist's, a genre's, a station's) as loading; the page swaps when the data arrives. One destination is never waited for: the page of the track the player holds, on a compact screen. It is the player expanded, drawn from what the queue already knows, so it takes the screen and its history entry at once, and what the catalog adds fills in place. On a desktop's screen the player does not expand (playback rule 13), and that page is waited for like any other.
2. The URL and the history move only when the screen does. A tap is an intention and lives in the Model; a history entry is a visit and is created when the destination takes the screen. Any number of taps that never get a reply leave history as it was. The exception is forced by the browser: on a direct link, a reload, Back or Forward the URL has already moved, so only the screen is held.
3. The latest intention wins, without a trace; replies to abandoned requests are dropped. A request with no answer after 10 seconds is a failure.
4. A page already loaded swaps in at once and refreshes in the background. A station's page is the one that does not refresh: a catalog deals a station's songs afresh each time it is asked, so asking again would put another list under the listener and Back would not find the one they left (rule 9). A station is dealt once for as long as the app is loaded.
5. The first paint shows placeholders in the shape of the page, never a blank area. On a fresh load the mark the page opens with stands over them until the page has something of its own to show ("How it looks and installs", 8).
6. A failure is said where the listener is. A track whose page fails to open from a list says so on its row, with "Try again", and the list stays as it is; so does an album asked from a row's menu. In the expanded player, a detail that fails to load says so on the face of the song's details, with "Try again", and the controls keep working. A page reached by its URL, and a section, say it on the page. So does an album chosen from a shelf: a tile has no more room to state a failure than a navigation item has, so the album's page takes the screen and says it, with "Try again" and the way back. An artist, a playlist, a genre and a station are only ever chosen from a tile, so theirs always does. So does a track's page chosen from the island's menu on a desktop's screen, which has no row to say it on (`onPage` on the `Intention`).
7. A failure that will not go away (a track, an album, an artist, a playlist, a genre or a station the catalog does not have) says so without "Try again": on its row, whose menu stops offering the page, or on its page with the way back. An album can be on many rows: the row it was asked from says so, and every row on that album stops offering it. An artist, a playlist, a genre and a station have no row, so each says it on its page.
8. Search keeps its previous results while a new term loads. Typing is the one navigation that is URL first, replacing its own entry, because the field's text lives in the URL. What each search found is kept under its term (`searched` in the Model), so a term searched for before shows its results at once, typed again or gone back to, and is asked for again behind them: after the typing pause, as any term is.
9. Going back to a list restores it exactly: same scroll position, same pictures already painted, nothing waiting on the network. Section pages stay mounted and hidden instead of being rebuilt, because Foldkit replaces the DOM when the view function at a position changes, and a rebuilt `<img>` repaints even when cached. An album's page is a list too, so it stays mounted behind a page reached from it (a song opened from one of its rows): one album at a time, the one on screen or else the nearest one behind it (`maybeAlbumIdInDocument` in `model.ts`). So are an artist's page, with their top songs and a shelf of their albums, and a playlist's, each kept the same way behind the album or the song reached from it (`maybeArtistIdInDocument`, `maybePlaylistIdInDocument`). So are a genre's page, with its top songs and its shelves, behind the song, the album, the artist or the playlist reached from it, and a station's, behind the song or the album (`maybeGenreIdInDocument`, `maybeStationIdInDocument`). Search is one page for every term: going back to an earlier search finds its results in the Model, with nothing waiting on the network, and the row the listener left it by is brought into view by the focus it is given; the scroll position itself is kept only for the search that was last on the page. Back to a list gives the focus to what the listener left it by: the row, or the tile of the album, the artist, the playlist, the genre or the station.
10. A picture never appears as an empty box when a rendition of it is already loaded: show that one first and let the larger one replace it.
11. On a narrow screen the navigation floats over the foot of the page: a menu of four sections (Home, New, Genres, Radio), and Search apart from it at the far end. A page scrolled down draws the menu back to a circle that shows where the listener is, and the island comes down into the row between the circle and Search. The menu draws back only to make that room: with nothing played there is no island, and the menu stays whole however far the page is scrolled. The top of the page brings the menu out again, and so does a press of the circle, where the page stands. A press made while the page is still running on from a flick keeps the menu out: that run was under way before the press and is no wish to send the menu away, so the following of the scroll waits for it to end (the page keeps still for a moment, or a finger is laid on it again) before it counts a way down. Choosing a section shows it whole; a track's page, an album's, an artist's, a playlist's, a genre's and a station's leave it as it was, so closing the track puts everything back. Under the expanded player there is no navigation: closing it is the way out. It is drawn again, out of reach, only while the player's sheet is being swiped away and uncovers it. Drawn back, the circle is one control: the item it shows is a picture of where the listener is, taken out of the order of focus, and the control over it is the one thing there to press or to focus. How the navigation stands is in the Model (`navigation`). The scroll is not: the Subscription in `subscription.ts` tells only the two moments that change it. Drawn back, the circle and Search are smaller than the menu is tall and stand on its centre line, where its two ends were; the island keeps its height. Each piece has its own times, different on the way out and on the way back, read frame by frame off the app they follow: they are in `styles.css` under the `dock-` names. None of them starts in the frame the navigation changes: that frame sets every piece of the dock moving and gives the island a new width, so it is the one that takes longest to draw, and a movement whose clock ran through it was first seen near its end, as a jump. What the change sets off is held at its start for that frame and let go on the next: the Mount `HoldDockMotion` (`mount.ts`), bound to the shell, holds the transitions, `DrawIslandFigure` holds what it starts for the island's figure, and both use `startWhenSeen` (`view/seenStart.ts`). The navigation stands a fixed distance from the foot of the page and adds no safe-area inset to it.

## Playback rules the app keeps

The player is its own part of the app (`packages/web/src/player/`, a Foldkit Submodel) and lives only in the client: the domain does not know that anything is playing.

1. Tapping a row plays it, and the list it was in becomes the queue: what is under the row is what comes next, and what is above it is behind it, where Previous leads. So an album has no control of its own to play it: any of its rows does, from that song on. On an album's page the list is the album; on an artist's, their top songs; on a playlist's, the playlist; on a genre's, its top songs; on a station's, the songs the station lists. A track's page opens from the menu at the end of its row; pressing the island expands it into the page of its track, on a compact screen (on a desktop's it does not expand: 13).
2. The island says only what the engine has said. A tap is a wish (`Acknowledged`, `Starting`); `Sounding` and `Waiting` come only from an engine report. Paused by the listener, stopped by the system and failed are three different states and look different.
3. Every order to the engine is numbered, and a report for an earlier order is dropped: the latest wish wins here too.
4. One audio element for the page's life. Navigation never touches it. Playing moves the route in one case only: an expanded player that goes on to another track rewrites its own history entry for that track, never adding one, so Back always closes it.
5. Sound starts inside the tap: `StartPlayback` calls `play()` as its first act, because phones refuse sound that does not come from a gesture.
6. Activity is shown only after 400 ms without sound, so a fast start never flashes a spinner.
7. A source that fails is asked of the catalog once more and retried from where it was. After that the island and the row say so, with a retry, and the queue never skips by itself.
8. The player knows no provider. `playableSource` in `player/model.ts` is the only reader of the catalog's audio field, and lengths and positions come from the engine, not from the catalog.
9. Expanded is derived, never stored. There is one route, `/track/:id`: the screen is the expanded player when the route's track is the player's current track and the screen is a compact one (`isPlayerExpanded` in `model.ts`), and the plain track page with a Play control and the island below otherwise. After a reload nothing is current, so a track's URL is its plain page.
10. Opening, the close control and the browser's Back are one view transition in two directions: the island and the expanded player share the name `player`, so the capsule grows into the screen and shrinks back, and the cover's name is on whichever of them is drawn. A track change inside the expanded player is no transition, and neither is a player whose sheet is already off the screen: the page behind it is what the listener sees (`viewTransition` reads the sheet's `reach`). A sheet let go of part of the way down is a transition like the others, from where the sheet is. The island's own picture does not travel with the box that grows into the sheet: it is kept at the foot of that box, where the island stands, gone early on the way out and back late on the way in, so it is never seen to rise up the screen or to come down from a place it never stood. Both pictures carry the one name, so the app names the two transitions (`player-opens` and `player-closes`, from `viewTransition` in main.ts) and the style sheet knows by them which picture is the island's. The expanded player has no blur: its picture is drawn again on every frame of a view transition, and a blur at a new size each time costs more than a frame has, which is what once made the island open in jumps.

    The expanded player started from the design (`design/album.pen`, "Isla abierta"; its motion in `design/motion/isla`) and has three faces (`pane` in the player's Model). On the song's, the cover fills the head of the sheet from edge to edge under a veil, with the track's title over its foot and nothing else over it; under it are the progress, with the time left counted down, the three controls, the main one 84 across, and under those two more: three dots at the near end, which open a menu (`player/moreMenu.ts`), and the way to the queue at the far one. The menu offers Go to album, which is followed as a link to the album's page would be (the player is held until the page can take the screen, and then shrinks to its island over it), and Song details, which turns the player to the face that says what the catalog knows of the track. Nothing more is laid out under the controls, so the song's face needs no scrolling. On the queue's face the cover is a thumbnail beside the title, in a low head right under the handle, and the songs to come are listed by their place in all that is played, each row ending in a handle where a list's row has the song's length. A song is taken up by its handle and carried to another place in the queue, or moved there with the keys (Foldkit UI's `DragAndDrop`, its Model in the session as `queueDrag`): the row it came from grows dim, the song is drawn at the height of the finger by the shell (`queueGhostView`, since the sheet is a box that what is in it is placed by), going up and down only and standing across where the list stands, since the finger's drift to either side says nothing in a list that runs one way, and a line shows where it would be put down. The rows do not move to show the place, which would move what the place is measured by. That is all that is done to the queue's order, with nothing to switch on first; the face of the song's details has the same head over what the catalog knows. The head is kept low and bare, with no name of the face over it, so that the room is for what the face lists. On both, the deck, the progress, the three controls and the two under them, stands at the foot of the sheet over what scrolls, the head itself leads back to the song, and the way to the queue, lit while the queue is open, leads to it and back. No face has a control of its own to close it. The deck is one piece on every face (`player/player.css` says where it stands), and what is in the sheet is put back at its top when the face changes (`ShowFaceFromTop`).

    The handle is the sheet's and no face's: a short bar at the very top of the sheet, as on any sheet that is swiped away, which stands there whatever face is shown and however far what is in the sheet has been scrolled. It says how the player is closed and, pressed, closes it. There is no other control for that. Nothing in the sheet, nor the bar an album's head draws back to, adds the inset of the screen's top: the pages are drawn under it already, and a browser that draws its own bar over the page (Brave on an iPhone) reports that inset to a page that is not under it, which once put the handle far down the sheet and made the album's bar twice its height.

    The cover travels: from the island's disc to the head of the sheet as the player opens (380 ms), and between the head and the thumbnail as it turns to the queue and back (280 ms, `player-queue-opens` and `player-queue-closes`), when the deck travels too. In the sheet the cover lies under the veil and the title, and a bare cover that simply arrived would lie over the title until the transition ended. So the song's face has the cover twice: the one that carries its name in a view transition, and over it one with no name, which stays in the sheet's picture. The one that travels lands on the other and fades into it. The box that grows from the island is the sheet's colour from the start, and what is in the sheet comes on it once the box is large enough to read. The times of all of it are in styles.css, worked out from the design's own account of the motion.

    The expanded player is drawn on a sheet (`packages/web/src/sheet/`, a Foldkit Submodel that follows React Aria's Sheet): the listener swipes it down to close it. The swipe is the browser's own scroll, not the app following a finger: the sheet stands on a stage inside a box that scrolls and snaps to the two places where the sheet is whole and where it is gone, so it has the momentum and the stretch of any scroll on the device (`sheet/geometry.ts`). What is in the sheet scrolls only while the sheet is whole, and from its top a swipe down goes on to the sheet. The Mount `FollowSheet` puts the box where the sheet is whole as it enters the document and tells the Model how much of the sheet is on screen (`reach`: `Whole`, `Partly`, `Gone`). The sheet also remembers a keyboard dismissal across the history callback. While the sheet is short of whole, the page the player was opened over is drawn behind it as it was left, with its navigation and its island, out of reach and with no names in a view transition, and a backdrop fades with the swipe by the sheet's own timeline (`sheet-backdrop` in styles.css). Let go of on its way down, far enough along or fast enough to be going, the sheet says `Leaving` while part of it is still on screen (`watchLettingGo` in `sheet/mount.ts` tells it, at the moment the finger lifts, and holds the sheet where it stands), and the player closes from there as its handle closes it: it is seen to draw back into its island from wherever it was let go, and does not slide on off the foot of the screen past an island it would then have nothing to do with. Should it get off the screen all the same, moved by something other than a finger, the sheet says `Left` and the player closes as its control closes it, back through the history, with nothing more to see; with no page behind, Home takes the screen at once. Escape says `RequestedClose` and restores the previous page immediately, or Home on direct entry.

11. The island is one figure, light on the light page and dark on the dark one, with one outline round three things: the cover, a disc at its left end that stands out a little above and below the body; the body, which curves to meet it; and a swell at the other end that holds the terracotta control that starts and stops the sound. Past the swell a lower tail holds Next while there is room for it; in the row with the navigation's circle and Search the tail is drawn in and the swell is the island's end. Going there is three movements, each setting off after the one before so that each can be followed: Next travels left with the end of the tail and in under the main control, which stays where it is; the island narrows; and it comes down into the row. Narrowing and coming down are one movement on a spring, and so is everything else that moves there: each piece goes a little past its place and comes back to it, which is what makes the whole read as one soft movement. The springs were read off the app they follow, frame by frame, from where the island's main control stood in each: coming out, the island goes 4% past its place in a fifth of a second and is at rest in under half of one; drawing back, 2.5% past in three tenths. The menu and Search are stiffer, 1.5% and half of one percent, since a circle that overshot would be seen to lose its shape. They are the `--ease-island-` and `--ease-menu-` curves in styles.css, written out as `linear()` because CSS has no spring. Each curve is a spring written out over its whole time, half a second coming out and a little over six tenths drawing back (`--dock-out`, `--dock-back`), and is played at that time: given less, it is the same spring played fast, past its place and back before the eye has seen it give, which is how the dock once came to move with no bounce at all. The island's width is the layout's and changes in one frame, so what is drawn of it is set back to the width it had and brought to the new one by script (`dockResizeMotion` in `player/mount.ts`), and that goes by the very clock its place goes by: the same time, curve and wait, read from the style sheet. Where the island's far end is seen is where it stands plus how wide it is drawn; when the width got there in a fraction of the spring's time, coming out threw that end, and the light on it, past the edge of the screen, and drawing back let it fall short and go on, which read as a great bounce. Coming out is the same three the other way round. The figure takes the cover's colours near the cover, as bright as they are. The island's words are one colour throughout, dark on the light island and light on the dark one, whatever the cover. Its edge is its own colour: at rest there is no border to see, and a soft shadow tells it from the page. What travels that edge is a light, not a line: a short copper beam with an almost white head, and a soft glow round it that spills to both sides of the edge. Only the engine's word that there is sound sets it going, at a slow and steady pace. It never starts or stops at once: put to rest (a pause, a stop, a failure, sound that ran out of data) it slows to a halt where it is and dims to an ember that stays there, and set going again it brightens and gathers speed from that same place. A tap not yet answered changes nothing, however long the start takes, so going on to another track does not halt it and a first track does not light it. Previous and the progress bar are the expanded player's; on a desktop's screen, where there is none, Previous stands in the island (13). The main control is one colour in both, the island's (`--color-island-control`), and the three glyphs of the transport are the design's. What a control alone cannot explain (stopped by the system, failed) stands in a few words where the artist was, and is said whole to assistive technology. A title too long for the island is a marquee: it waits, travels across once with its copy following it in, and waits again. The island changing width under it disturbs nothing, since a pass goes as far as the title is long and not as its place is wide: the wait or the pass under way goes on, and a title that comes to fit in the middle of a pass ends that pass before it stands (`Finishing`). All of this is in the architecture, with nothing of its own outside it. What the light does (`light`) and where the title is in its waits and passes (`title`) are in the player's Model, the waits and the passes are Commands, and the view draws the figure, the light and the title from them. What only the document knows reaches it through Foldkit's `Mount`, an effect bound to one element (`player/mount.ts`): `MeasureTitle` tells the Model whether the title fits and how far a pass takes it; `DrawIslandFigure` writes the island's outline, worked out in `view/islandOutline.ts`, to the figure's paths in the very frame the island changes width, since a width sent through the Model would be drawn a frame late, and in that same frame puts the light back where the listener saw it, because the light is placed in shares of the outline and an outline that grows or shrinks would carry it along the edge; `StartIslandLight` sets the light's animations to what the Model says as the island enters the document. From then on `update` gives the Command `ChangeIslandLightPace` whenever the Model's `light` changes, and the light's pace changes little by little; a change that overtakes one under way carries on from the pace the light had reached. While a song changes, the island is one side of a view transition whose box is cut with `clip-path` drawn a little outside it, because the cover and the swell stand out of the island's box. The light and the shadow have view transition names of their own (`island-light` and `island-shadow`, in styles.css): a transition fades a still picture of the island into the live one, and a light inside that picture would stand still for as long as the fade lasts, as a shadow inside it would be cut where the picture is cut.

12. A row is one surface and ends in one control, three dots, that opens a menu of what else can be done with its track. A press on the row lights all of it, the control's place included, and a lit surface stands free: the line under its row and the line under the row above are not drawn, since a straight line would run into its rounded corners (`track-row` in styles.css). The menu offers, in this order: Play next, which puts the track right after the current one; Add to queue, which puts it after all the rest; Go to album, which is followed as a link to the page of the album the track is on would be; and Song details, which is followed as a link to the track's page would be (navigation rule 1). Go to album is offered on every row but those of the album's own page, for as long as the catalog has the album. A track is queued only once something is playing and is never the current track, the queue holds a track once among those to come, and queueing changes nothing that sounds. What was chosen is answered where it was chosen. Choosing a page, the song's or the album's, leaves the menu open: the item that was pressed turns its mark while the page is on its way, and if the page fails to open the item says so and a second press of it tries again, so nobody opens the menu twice to retry. The menu goes with the list when the page takes the screen. With the menu closed, the row's control turns instead, and the row says the failure under itself. Queueing closes the menu, and the control shows a tick for a moment, said in words to assistive technology only. What the catalog said of a track is kept under the track's id, so every row for that track reads it. An album is on many rows, so what the catalog said of it does not tell which row was used: the Model notes the row each album was last asked from (`albumRows`), and that row alone turns its control, or says under itself that the album could not be opened, with its own "Try again", or that it is not available. An album chosen from a shelf has no such row, and its tile turns instead. The menu is Foldkit UI's `Menu`, one Model for each row kept under the row's id (`rowMenus`; a menu never touched is not kept), and `rowMenu.ts` says what it can offer. It is not modal: its backdrop covers the screen and hears the press that closes it. The menu under the expanded player's controls is closed the same way, by a press anywhere else, the control that opened it among that: its backdrop is drawn over the player's sheet, which stands at the height a row's backdrop has, and on a compact screen it is closed for good once the player is its island again. On a desktop's screen the menu is the island's own (13).

13. On a desktop's screen, 64rem and wider, the player does not expand: there is room for three columns, the navigation, the page and the queue (`design/desktop.pen`). Whether the screen is one is in the Model (`screen`, `Compact` or `Desktop`, in `screen.ts`), told by the Subscription `screens` as the app starts and whenever the window crosses that width. It is the one width the Model holds, because it changes what the player is and not only where its pieces are drawn. The navigation going to the side at 48rem is still the style sheet's alone, and between the two widths the player is a sheet.

    There the island's words are no link, and it has controls of its own for what the sheet would have held: the way to the queue and the three dots of the player's menu before its transport, and Previous in the transport. The menu offers Go to album and Song details. Song details is followed as a link to the track's page would be: on this screen that is always the plain page, waited for like any other, and with no row to state a failure on, it takes the screen and says it. There is no progress bar on this screen yet.

    The queue opens in a column of its own past the page (`queueColumn` in the player's Model; it is the player's and no session's, so it stays open across pages and across lists). At its head are its name and the control that closes it, which hands the focus back to the island's; under them the song that plays next, on a card with its cover, and those that play later, each with its place, who it is by, its length and its handle. It is the same queue, moved the same way: the card and the list are one place to put a song down in. It is no route. Opening and closing it moves neither the URL nor the history, and the page beside it is the page on screen, in reach. The column, the page and the navigation each scroll by themselves.

    The column is between 20 and 26.5rem wide, by the screen's width, and the navigation is 11rem there, so the page never has less room than it has beside the navigation at 48rem. A page whose layout depends on its width goes by its own (`@container` on a track's page), not the screen's. The column comes in from its side and goes back off it as a view transition (`queue-column-opens` and `queue-column-closes`): the page has its new width in the one frame, since a width that changed little by little would lay every shelf out again on every frame, and the island goes to its new place with the column. Crossing the width with the window moves nothing in the history and is no transition: the page of the track the player holds is the sheet on one side of it and that track's plain page on the other.

## How it looks and installs

1. The palette is aubergine, built on the two colours of the mark: the deep aubergine of the speaker (`#46203f`) and the pale orchid it stands on (`#efdfec`). Every colour is a token in `styles.css`, light and dark, and no class names a colour of its own.
2. A shelf snaps: a swipe along it comes to rest with a tile at its leading edge.
3. An album's cover travels between its tile on a shelf and the head of its page, as a track's does between its row and its page: the tile's picture and the page's carry one name on the page on screen (`albumCoverTransitionStyle`). On the page the name is on the whole head, the cover with the title and the facts over it (`albumHeadFlightAttributes`), so the words grow out of the tile with the cover and shrink back into it with it; a cover that travelled alone would land bare, and the words would appear over it a step behind. The way back lies over the head and comes in on the head's clock (`album-return` in `page/album.css`). An artist's picture and a playlist's travel the same way (`artistPictureTransitionStyle`, `playlistPictureTransitionStyle`).
4. A change of page is seen to happen, and how says what kind of change it was (`viewTransition` in `main.ts` names it, and `styles.css` draws it). From one section to another is a change of place: the page gives way where it stands and the other comes in its place, the two never seen together. The navigation's mark does not wait for that: it travels from the item the listener left to the one they chose as they choose it (`maybeMarkedDestination` in `view.ts`; `dock-mark`, one piece for the whole menu and not each item's own ground), and the chosen item's own icon breathes there for as long as its page takes (`nav-breathes`), with no spinner in its place. A mark that moved only once the page had come would read as the app being slow to hear. Which page is on screen is still said by `aria-current`, which moves when the page does. Opening a page from a list is a step deeper: the picture the two share travels between them (3) and, where the list has no picture of it to carry (a song's page from a row's menu on an album, which has no thumbnails), the page itself comes over the list from the far side while the list steps back under it, and goes back off it the same way. Which of the two it is only the document knows, so `entry.ts` decides it, and notes which pages came over so that they go back as they came. A move through the history that the browser drew itself (the swipe from the screen's edge that goes back or forward) is not drawn a second time, and nothing else is set moving for it either: the browser says so on its `popstate`, `entry.ts` says it on the document until the app has drawn the page that answers the move, and for that drawing there is no view transition, every CSS transition is held, and what moves by script stands still (`view/shownByBrowser.ts`). It is held by that drawing and not by a clock: after such a swipe Safari keeps the page from drawing for a third of a second or so.
5. An album's head draws back to a bar as its page is scrolled, by the scroll and not by a clock. The cover is never seen to shrink: for all of the way but the very last it keeps its size, goes up with the page and only grows dim, until it is clear; then, as the bar is about to arrive, the thumbnail comes in its place there, a little large, and settles to its size and its brightness. A cover that grew smaller while it could still be seen read as a picture being squeezed, however late it began and however dim it was. The words go up with the page and grow smaller towards the place the bar's title has, where they give way to it, so the title is seen to rise and settle in the bar. There is one cover to see wherever the page is scrolled. Every album's songs are at least as tall as the screen under the bar, however few they are, so that any album can be scrolled far enough for its head to finish: a page too short for that was left with its cover on its way for good. The bar is as high as a bar of the system's (`--album-bar-inner` in `page/album.css`) and takes over only once the cover has landed.
6. The mark of the track the player holds, on its row, holds its spinner and its bars both, one over the other, and the one not shown is small and clear: a start slow enough to turn the spinner becomes sound where it stands (`now-mark` in `styles.css`). The queue's rows are a list's rows, with the same surface that a press lights (`track-row`).
7. A tile whose page is on its way says so with its own picture, and nothing is laid over it: the picture is a sleeve, which moves a little aside, and its record comes part of the way out from behind it and turns for as long as the page takes (`tile-record` in `styles.css`; the alternatives it was chosen from are in `design/motion/tile-loaders.html`). It is clear, not hidden, while it is behind its sleeve: a page kept behind the one on screen is hidden as a whole, and a piece of it that said it was visible would be seen through it. The record is no part of the piece that travels to the page's head: the picture goes, and the record stays with the shelf. Under reduced motion it is out and still.
8. Bafle installs to a home screen. The manifest, the icons and the mark are in `packages/web/public` (their sources in `design/`). While an iPhone starts the installed app it shows a launch picture of the screen's exact size, light or dark (`public/splash`, drawn from the mark); while the app itself loads, in any browser, the same mark stands in the page (`#splash` in `index.html`) and gives way when the first page has something of its own to show: what the catalog said of it, or that it could not be asked. The app's first drawing is only placeholders, and the mark stands over them: the shell says `data-launching` for as long as the Model's `launch` is `Launching`, and the mark reads it. Launched is for good, so a page that waits after that, a first search among them, waits in the app. There is no service worker: the app needs the network to be of any use, and nothing is kept for offline.

The engine is the `AudioEngine` service (`player/audioEngine.ts`); `packages/web/src/entry.ts` names the implementation in `resources`, and tests name a fake.
