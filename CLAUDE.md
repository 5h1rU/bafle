# Bafle

A music player whose music provider sits behind an Effect service, so the provider (Audius today) can be swapped without touching the model or the app.

## Stack

- **Model**: `@alchemy.run/domain` (domain-driven design on Effect) in `packages/domain`. It imports only `@alchemy.run/domain` and `effect`.
- **Server**: a Cloudflare Worker in `packages/server` that hosts the model and holds the provider adapters.
- **App**: Foldkit (The Elm Architecture on Effect) in `packages/web`.
- **Infrastructure**: Alchemy, one stack in `alchemy.run.ts`, deployed to Cloudflare. Production is `https://bafle.app` (a zone in the same account, the app Worker's custom domain; `www.bafle.app` redirects to it). The Api Worker stays on workers.dev, since the app reaches it through a service binding.

## Skills to load before working

- `alchemy-domain` for anything in `packages/domain` or `packages/server`. The copy in `.claude/skills/` comes from the installed `@alchemy.run/domain` package, so it matches the APIs the code compiles against.
- `foldkit-skills:foldkit` for anything in `packages/web`, with `packages/web/FOLDKIT.md` as the conventions for the installed Foldkit release.

Effect v4, Foldkit and Alchemy v2 are new and change often. Read the installed types in `node_modules` and the skills' references instead of writing their APIs from memory.

## How the app is tested

The app's tests run in a real browser (Vitest's browser mode, on Playwright's Chromium), not in a stand-in for a document: `packages/web/vitest.config.ts` builds the app for them as it is built to run. A machine that has never run them needs the browser once: `pnpm --filter @bafle/web exec playwright install chromium`.

Three kinds, all under `packages/web/test`. Stories drive `update` and scenes drive the view, as Foldkit's own do. The tests under `test/visual` look at the app: each hands a Model to the app's own view, draws it on a phone's screen or a desktop's with the style sheet the app ships (`looksAs` in `test/visual/draw.ts`), and compares what is seen with a picture kept beside the test (`toMatchScreenshot`, in `__screenshots__`). A change that is not meant to be seen leaves every picture as it was; one that is meant to be seen is looked at and then kept. The pictures are drawn in CI and nowhere else (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): in Playwright's image at the release the catalog pins, with Inter as the system's type (`test/visual/fonts.conf`), since the app's type is the system's and the comparison allows no pixel off. To keep a change that is meant to be seen, run that workflow by hand with "Draw the pictures again" on the branch: it draws them (`pnpm draw-pictures`) and commits them there, and they are looked at in the commit.

Anywhere but in CI, a Mac or a Linux machine alike, the tests compare with pictures of that machine's own, drawn there under `__screenshots__/local` and never kept (`.gitignore`; `resolveScreenshotPath` in `packages/web/vitest.config.ts`, which tells CI by `GITHUB_ACTIONS`): another machine draws the same page a few levels of colour apart, even in CI's image, so they are no reference for CI's, nor CI's for them, and a Linux machine's would otherwise bear the names of CI's and be drawn over them. They are the way to see a change before it is pushed. `pnpm draw-pictures` draws them from the code as it stands, before the change; `pnpm test` after it compares with them, and what differs is left in `packages/web/.vitest`, what was seen beside how it differs. A run that finds no picture of the machine's own draws it and fails, so the first run on a machine fails every visual test. They hold for as long as nothing else changes the page: after a pull, or the catalog's Playwright moving on, they are drawn again before the next change. What is kept is still drawn in CI.

Every push to main and every pull request is formatted (`pnpm format:check`, oxfmt), linted, typed, built (`pnpm build`) and tested by that workflow. `pnpm format` lays the code out.

## Where the provider is chosen

`packages/domain/src/Catalog/MusicCatalog.external.ts` declares what Bafle asks of a catalog, in its own words. Each adapter is the only code that knows its provider: `packages/server/src/Audius/`, the one in use, and `packages/server/src/Deezer/`, kept since Deezer stopped letting its audio be played outside its own apps. `packages/server/src/BafleWorker.ts` names the adapter in use: to change provider, write another `MusicCatalog.toLayer(...)` and name it there.

What a provider lacks, its adapter makes out of what it has, so that the app never knows. Audius plays the whole track, from an address of its own that never runs out (`audioOf` in `Audius/translate.ts`), and has no editors, no stations and no genre pictures:

- An album is one of Audius's playlists that it calls one. A track on no album is a single, the album of that one track, under the track's id (`single-…`), so every track's row can lead to an album.
- Genres are Audius's upload genres that are music (`AUDIUS_GENRES`), the 24 with the most filed under them (`GENRES_SHOWN`), each under a slug of its name, with the cover of what is played most in it this week.
- Each genre has three stations, what is played most in it this week, this month and of all time (`WINDOWS`), each dealt afresh from the top hundred of that window when it is asked for (`week-electronic`, `stationOf`).
- Audius names another of its nodes for a picture in every answer, so every picture is drawn from its own node (`AUDIUS_PICTURES`) and is the same address wherever it is listed.
- Every question to Audius carries the bearer token of Bafle's app on Audius (`AUDIUS_BEARER_TOKEN`, read from `.env` and bound to the Worker as a secret), which Audius answers ten times a second. A list with a question for each genre asks two at a time, to stay under that.

Each adapter says where a picture's likeness is worked out from (`likenessSourceOf`): Deezer draws any picture as a PNG of 32 pixels when asked, and Audius draws JPEG only and at 150 pixels at the least, so the server has Audius's drawn again as a small PNG by Cloudflare Images (navigation rule 10).

The lists that are the same for every listener (what Home recommends, what is played most, the genres, the stations) are kept in Workers KV once the provider has answered (`packages/server/src/Kept/`, `KeptReplies`), with the isolate's memory in front of it: a visit is answered from what is kept, and when that is no longer fresh the provider is asked behind the reply (`waitUntil`), so no visit waits for it but the first. How long each is kept is the adapter's to say, since only it knows what its replies carry: Deezer signs the address of a song's audio to expire after a quarter of an hour, so it keeps what is played most for minutes (`TRENDING_KEPT_FOR`) and the lists with no audio for a day (`LISTS_KEPT_FOR`); Audius's addresses never run out, so it keeps every list for a day, and what is played most is fresh for ten minutes (`TRENDING_FRESH_FOR`). Audius's lists are kept under names of their own (`audius:featured`), so it never hands over what Deezer's kept. A page of one thing (a track, an album, a search) is asked of the provider every time.

## How the app is put together

Foldkit's own conventions are in `packages/web/FOLDKIT.md`, and its rules are linted (`pnpm lint`: oxlint with `@foldkit/oxlint-plugin`, pinned in the catalog to the release that goes with the `foldkit` installed; `packages/web/.oxlintrc.json`). A change is not done while the linter has anything to say.

A part of the app that owns a state machine is a Submodel: its own Model, Messages, update and, where it draws, a view, in a folder of its own. A part that only draws what its owner holds is a view function. The pages are view functions: they draw the app's Model and have no Messages of their own.

- `player/`: the player. It tells the app what it cannot do itself (`OutMessage`: the album or the song's details chosen from its menu, another track become the current one), and the app asks things of it through what it exports (`choose`, `enqueue`, `collapse`, `closeMenu`, `showDetails`), never by building one of its Messages.
- `player/island/`: the island, inside the player: its light, its two lines of words, its figure and its Mounts. The player tells it what the sound is doing and that its track changed (`informSound`, `informTrackChanged`), and draws what is its own on it, the cover and the controls, in the places the island's view gives it (`toBody`, `toControls`, `toFoot`).
- `marquee/`: a line of words that travels when it does not fit. The island's title and the line under it are two of them.
- `player/queue/`: the queue as a thing on screen: the song of it being moved, and its column. It tells the player that a song was chosen or put down elsewhere; the order is the player's.
- `rows/`: what the rows of every list keep between them: the menus at their ends, and the mark of what was just done. It tells the app what was chosen from a row's menu.
- `sheet/`: the sheet the expanded player is drawn on.

The same holds between any two of them. A child says what happened with an `OutMessage` and its owner folds it (`Update.foldChild` with `foldOutMessage`); an owner that needs something of a child calls a helper the child exports; nobody reads another's Messages to find out what it did, changes a field of another's Model, or compares another's Model before and after. A wrapper Message (`Got*Message`) carries the child's Message and what routes it (an id), nothing else. A module's `index.ts` only re-exports, and a Model or Message module imports its children's `model.ts` and `message.ts`, not their `index.ts`, which would bring their views in with them and the app's Messages round in a circle.

## How the app is styled

Tailwind, and one rule about what is not Tailwind. They are requirements, as the navigation's are: the app once said the same thing in a class list and in a sheet that undid it, and nobody could tell which of the two a piece was drawn by.

1. What a piece looks like is said in the class list of the view function that draws it, with utilities. A view function is the component: a look that is used twice is a function called twice, not a class in a sheet.
2. CSS written by hand is for what a class list cannot say, and each sheet holds one such thing: the pictures a view transition makes and how they move (`view/pageTransitions.css`, `player/transitions.css`, `player/island.css`); a movement by the scroll, its timeline and what each piece does along it (`page/album.css`, `view/turning.css`); a movement of several pieces at once, each with its own times (`view/dock.css`, `view/record.css`); keyframes that a style written by script names (`sheet/sheet.css`). A sheet is written beside the view that owns it, and `styles.css`, the one entry, brings every sheet in with `@import ... layer(components)`, in a written order. That layer is under the utilities': a sheet cannot win over a class list. What a sheet finds a piece by is a class that says nothing of its look (`dock-island`, `album-hero`, `turning-row`), kept in the piece's class list beside its utilities. Where a piece stands in each state, and what it looks like there, is the class list's all the same: a state is a variant (`minimized:`, `keyboard:`, `can-hover:`, `fine-pointer:`, `tall:`, `search-wide:`, `scroll-timelines:`, each defined in `styles.css`; `has-[…]`, `group-data-…`, `in-data-…` for what a neighbour or an ancestor says of itself), and an animation a utility plays has its keyframes in `@theme` (`animate-breathe`).
3. A sheet never sets a property that a utility sets on the same piece. A piece whose look differs from place to place says which with a variant, a closed union (`RowLead`, `ShelfLayout`, `PageEdges`, `IconControlLook`, `QueueFace`), and each variant is a whole class list in a table: no view is handed class names to add, and no class list is put together from parts of names, since Tailwind only finds a class written out whole. The one thing a view may add to a component's class list is where the piece stands among its neighbours, a margin. No class list holds two utilities that set one property under the same variants, one to undo the other: which of them a browser heeds goes by the order Tailwind writes its rules in and not by the list's, so the two ways a piece can be are two whole class lists, of which the view takes one (`MARK_PIECES` in `view/trackRow.ts`).
4. A value used in more than one place is a token in `@theme` (`styles.css`): every colour, the sizes of type between the scale's steps (`text-13`), the weights and shadows the designs name, a measure two pieces share (`px-album-gutter`, `h-album-bar`). A breakpoint is Tailwind's, in a class list (`md:`, `@2xl:`, `@min-[40rem]/search:`) and in a sheet (`@variant md`), never a width written out again. The ring of a control the keys have brought the focus to is one utility (`focus-ring`, `focus-ring-inset`).

What the app is built from, each said once:

- `view/control.ts`: a control that is a mark alone (`iconButtonView`, `iconControlClass`) and a pill with words (`pillButtonView`, `pillClass`).
- `view/menu.ts`: what every menu looks like, and what can be done with a track, by one name and one mark wherever it is offered (`ACTION_LOOKS`).
- `view/trackList.ts` and `view/trackRow.ts`: every list of songs. A page says what its list is as a value (`TrackSection`, or `TrackList` for the list alone) and that value draws the list and what stands in its place before the tracks are known. The row's frame (`rowClass`, `rowSurfaceClass`, `ROW_PLAY_CLASS`) is the queue's rows' too.
- `view/shelf.ts`: a shelf, a grid of tiles, and the tile, built for every kind of thing by `tileOf`.
- `view/page.ts`: the two frames a page is drawn in, a section's (`sectionPageView`) and that of a page of one thing (`detailPageView`, with `detailHeadView`), and `sectionsView` for what a page lists.
- `view/artwork.ts`: a picture in its box, and what a kind's picture travels as (`pictureFlightStyle`).

A change to any of this is looked at before it is kept: the pictures under `test/visual` are of every page on both screens, and a change that is not meant to be seen leaves them all as they were.

## Navigation rules the app keeps

The app exists to show loading done well, so these are requirements, not style. Its pages are the five sections (Home `/`, New `/new`, Genres `/genres`, Radio `/radio`, Search `/search`), a track's page (`/track/:id`), an album's page (`/album/:id`), an artist's page (`/artist/:id`), a playlist's page (`/playlist/:id`), a genre's page (`/genre/:id`) and a station's page (`/station/:id`), all in `packages/web/src/route.ts`. Genres shows every genre the catalog files music under, as tiles; a genre's page shows what is played most in it: its top songs, and shelves of albums, artists and playlists. Radio shows the catalog's stations on shelves, one for each genre that has any; a station's page lists the songs it plays now, as a playlist's lists its own. Every tile leads to the page of what it shows:

1. Going to a page whose data is not loaded holds the current page and marks what was chosen (the navigation item, the control at the end of the row, or the tile: an album's, an artist's, a playlist's, a genre's, a station's) as loading; the page swaps when the data arrives. The data has arrived once the pictures the page opens with are ready too: the catalog's reply is held until they are loaded and decoded, for 300 ms at most (`firstPicturesOf` in `view/firstPictures.ts`, `PICTURE_WAIT` in `command.ts`), so that the page takes the screen with them painted and none is seen as an empty box that fills a frame later. Until then the Model says the page is on its way, as it did while the catalog was asked. The views draw those same pictures `AtOnce`: asked for first, and decoded as they are drawn, which costs nothing for a picture already decoded. One destination is never waited for: the page of the track the player holds, on a compact screen. It is the player expanded, drawn from what the queue already knows, so it takes the screen and its history entry at once, and what the catalog adds fills in place. On a desktop's screen the player does not expand (playback rule 13), and that page is waited for like any other. The two waits a page is held for are measured apart, the catalog's reply and the pictures, with whether the pictures were ready or their time ran out. A wait is an Effect span (`asWait` in `timing.ts`), and the Tracer named in `entry.ts` writes each to the browser's User Timing as it ends, under the name of the Command it was part of (`AlbumDetail: pictures`): they are read in the browser's own tools, or with `performance.getEntriesByType("measure")`.
2. The URL and the history move only when the screen does. A tap is an intention and lives in the Model; a history entry is a visit and is created when the destination takes the screen. Any number of taps that never get a reply leave history as it was. The exception is forced by the browser: on a direct link, a reload, Back or Forward the URL has already moved, so only the screen is held.
3. The latest intention wins, without a trace; replies to abandoned requests are dropped. A request with no answer after 10 seconds is a failure.
4. A page already loaded swaps in at once and refreshes in the background. So that the sections are, they are asked for ahead of the listener: once the first page has the screen (`launch` is `Launched`), the sections the Model does not hold (Home, New, Genres, Radio; Search has nothing to ask until it is typed in) are asked for one at a time (`ahead` in the Model, `askNextAhead` in `main.ts`), each with the pictures it opens with, and each is in the document, hidden, from the moment it is held. Asking ahead is no intention: nothing on screen says it is happening, a failure is said nowhere and leaves the section to be asked for when it is chosen, and the listener goes before it. A section chosen while it is on its way takes the screen with the reply that was ahead of them. Nothing is asked ahead for a listener whose browser says they are saving data, nor after a first page that could not be asked. A station's page is the one that does not refresh: a catalog deals a station's songs afresh each time it is asked, so asking again would put another list under the listener and Back would not find the one they left (rule 9). A station is dealt once for as long as the app is loaded.
5. The first paint shows placeholders in the shape of the page, never a blank area. Search's shape is its field in the middle of the page ("How it looks and installs", 13). On a fresh load the mark the page opens with stands over them until the page has something of its own to show ("How it looks and installs", 8).
6. A failure is said where the listener is. A track whose page fails to open from a list says so on its row, and the list stays as it is; so does an album asked from a row's menu. A row says it without growing: its last control turns to a mark in the colour of what went wrong (`--color-danger`), and the menu that control opens says why on the item that failed, in that colour, and the item pressed again is the way to try again. Its thumbnail, where it has one, is a sleeve whose record comes out and breaks, as a tile's does. A row is two lines whatever became of its track: there is no line under it and no control of its own to try again. A page chosen from a tile (an album's, an artist's, a playlist's, a genre's, a station's, and the artist Search found first, on their card) that the catalog could not be asked for stays behind its tile, and the URL and the history with it: the record that turned while the page was on its way breaks in two, the picture takes the colour of what went wrong, "Couldn't load" and "Try again" stand over it, and the tile itself is the way to try again, its record whole again (`maybeFailedTile` in the Model; `recordView` and `failureOverView` in `view/shelf.ts`). Why is said to assistive technology, and the focus stays on the tile. There is one such tile at most, and whatever the listener does next forgets it. In the expanded player, a detail that fails to load says so on the face of the song's details, with "Try again", and the controls keep working. A page reached by its URL, by a reload, by Back or Forward, and a section chosen from the navigation, say it on the page: in the middle of the room the page has under its head, over the signal cut short, with "Try again" (`failureView`). So does a page chosen from the island's menu, an album's or, on a desktop's screen, a track's, which has no row or tile to say it on (`onPage` on the `Intention`).
7. A failure that will not go away (a track, an album, an artist, a playlist, a genre or a station the catalog does not have) says so without a way to try again: on its row, whose menu lists the page as not available and not to be pressed, or on its page. An album can be on many rows: the row it was asked from says so, and every other row on that album stops offering it. An artist, a playlist, a genre and a station have no row, so each says it on its page. Chosen from a tile, it is no failure of the tile's: the page takes the screen to say so, since there is nothing for the tile to try again.
8. Search keeps its previous results while a new term loads. It also keeps what its field held when the listener left it (`maybeSearchText` in the Model), answered or not, and the navigation's Search leads back to that: Search is found as it was left, the same text in the field over what the catalog found for it, and is asked again behind itself as any page held is. Emptied before it was left, it is found empty. Typing is the one navigation that is URL first, replacing its own entry, because the field's text lives in the URL. What each search found is kept under its term (`searched` in the Model), so a term searched for before shows its results at once, typed again or gone back to, and is asked for again behind them: after the typing pause, as any term is.
9. Going back to a list restores it exactly: same scroll position, same pictures already painted, nothing waiting on the network. Section pages stay mounted and hidden instead of being rebuilt, because Foldkit replaces the DOM when the view function at a position changes, and a rebuilt `<img>` repaints even when cached. An album's page is a list too, so it stays mounted behind a page reached from it (a song opened from one of its rows): one album at a time, the one on screen or else the nearest one behind it (`maybeAlbumIdInDocument` in `model.ts`). So are an artist's page, with their top songs and a shelf of their albums, and a playlist's, each kept the same way behind the album or the song reached from it (`maybeArtistIdInDocument`, `maybePlaylistIdInDocument`). So are a genre's page, with its top songs and its shelves, behind the song, the album, the artist or the playlist reached from it, and a station's, behind the song or the album (`maybeGenreIdInDocument`, `maybeStationIdInDocument`). Search is one page for every term: going back to an earlier search finds its results in the Model, with nothing waiting on the network, and the row the listener left it by is brought into view by the focus it is given; the scroll position itself is kept only for the search that was last on the page. Back to a list gives the focus to what the listener left it by: the row, or the tile of the album, the artist, the playlist, the genre or the station.
10. A picture never appears as an empty box when a rendition of it is already loaded: show that one first and let the larger one replace it. When none is, its likeness stands in its place: a blurred image of its colours and shapes, drawn as the ground of the picture's box (`view/likeness.ts`) and covered by the picture when it comes. The likeness is a ThumbHash the catalog sends with the picture's addresses (`placeholder` on `Artwork`), so it is there the moment they are, with nothing more to ask for. The server works it out from the picture at 32 pixels, as a PNG read with nothing but the platform, and keeps it for good in Workers KV (`packages/server/src/Placeholder/`). A catalog that draws no such PNG (Audius draws JPEG) has its picture drawn as one by the Images binding first (`smallPngOf`), which writes a picture with no colour as a grey PNG and one with few colours as a palette, so the server reads every kind of PNG but those of 16 bits and the interlaced (`Placeholder/png.ts`): a transformation Cloudflare counts once a picture, since the likeness is kept, and that fails, leaving the picture without its likeness, once the month's allowance is spent. A reply carries the likenesses already known and never waits for the others: those are worked out after it has gone (`waitUntil`), as many as the longest page draws (`LIKENESSES_PER_REPLY`, 64), six at a time, so a picture seen for the first time may come without one.
11. On a narrow screen the navigation floats over the foot of the page: a menu of four sections (Home, New, Genres, Radio), and Search apart from it at the far end. A page scrolled down draws the menu back to a circle that shows where the listener is, and the island comes down into the row between the circle and Search. The menu draws back only to make that room: with nothing played there is no island, and the menu stays whole however far the page is scrolled. The top of the page brings the menu out again, and so does a press of the circle, where the page stands. On Search the circle shows a section the listener is not on, the one they came from, and there a press of it also goes to that section, as its item in the menu would (`ClickedShowNavigation`; `circleSectionOf` in `route.ts`): it is not one press to bring the menu out and another to leave Search. A press made while the page is still running on from a flick keeps the menu out: that run was under way before the press and is no wish to send the menu away, so the following of the scroll waits for it to end (the page keeps still for a moment, or a finger is laid on it again) before it counts a way down. Choosing a section shows it whole; a track's page, an album's, an artist's, a playlist's, a genre's and a station's leave it as it was, so closing the track puts everything back. A page gone back to finds the navigation as it was beside it, by where the page stands: drawn back over a page left below its top, whole over one at it (`MeasureReturnedPage`, read off the document once the page is drawn), whatever the pages between did with it. Only the page on screen brings the menu out by reaching its top: a page kept behind it that scrolls says nothing. Under the expanded player there is no navigation: closing it is the way out. It is drawn again, out of reach, only while the player's sheet is being swiped away and uncovers it. Drawn back, the circle is one control: the item it shows is a picture of where the listener is, taken out of the order of focus, and the control over it is the one thing there to press or to focus. How the navigation stands is in the Model (`navigation`). The scroll is not: the Subscription in `subscription.ts` tells only the two moments that change it. Drawn back, the circle and Search are smaller than the menu is tall and stand on its centre line, where its two ends were; the island keeps its height. Each piece has its own times, different on the way out and on the way back, read frame by frame off the app they follow: they are in `view/dock.css` under the `dock-` names. None of them starts in the frame the navigation changes: that frame sets every piece of the dock moving and gives the island a new width, so it is the one that takes longest to draw, and a movement whose clock ran through it was first seen near its end, as a jump. What the change sets off is held at its start for that frame and let go on the next: the Mount `HoldDockMotion` (`mount.ts`), bound to the shell, holds the transitions, the island's Mount `DrawFigure` holds what it starts for the island's figure, and both use `startWhenSeen` (`view/seenStart.ts`). The navigation stands a fixed distance from the foot of the page and adds no safe-area inset to it.

## Playback rules the app keeps

The player is its own part of the app (`packages/web/src/player/`, a Foldkit Submodel) and lives only in the client: the domain does not know that anything is playing.

1. Tapping a row plays it, and the list it was in becomes the queue: what is under the row is what comes next, and what is above it is behind it, where Previous leads. So an album has no control of its own to play it: any of its rows does, from that song on. On an album's page the list is the album; on an artist's, their top songs; on a playlist's, the playlist; on a genre's, its top songs; on a station's, the songs the station lists. A track's page opens from the menu at the end of its row; pressing the island expands it into the page of its track, on a compact screen (on a desktop's it does not expand: 13).
2. The island says only what the engine has said. A tap is a wish (`Acknowledged`, `Starting`); `Sounding` and `Waiting` come only from an engine report. Paused by the listener, stopped by the system and failed are three different states and look different. The bar of the sound's progress is drawn from what the engine says too, and is moved by the style sheet, not by the app. The engine's own reports come when the second changes, as late as its clock happens to run, about four times a second and never on the second: a bar drawn from those alone was more than a second behind the sound and halted nearly every second. So while a bar is on screen and follows the sound (`drawsSoundProgress` in `model.ts`: the expanded player, or the island on a desktop's screen, with sound and no place the listener wants), the Subscription `soundPlace` asks the engine where the sound is, at once and then every 250 ms (`soundPlaces` and `SOUND_PLACE_EVERY` in `player/subscription.ts`; `now` on the engine), to the fraction, and each answer is heeded as any report is. The bar is sent to each place by a transition of 350 ms (`seekBarView`), longer than the places are apart, so it is still on its way when the next is said: it moves at the pace of the sound, a third of a second behind it, and no code of the app's runs for a frame of it. Asking the engine for every frame was tried: each answer is a Message, and drew the whole app again sixty times a second. The bar does not travel to a place the listener wants, where it stands at once, nor from one track to the start of the next. What is told of the place, the time played, the time left and the slider's value, counts whole seconds.
3. Every order to the engine is numbered, and a report for an earlier order is dropped: the latest wish wins here too.
4. One audio element for the page's life. Navigation never touches it. Playing moves the route in one case only: an expanded player that goes on to another track rewrites its own history entry for that track, never adding one, so Back always closes it.
5. Sound starts inside the tap: `StartPlayback` calls `play()` as its first act, because phones refuse sound that does not come from a gesture.
6. Activity is shown only after 400 ms without sound, so a fast start never flashes a spinner.
7. A source that fails is asked of the catalog once more and retried from where it was. After that the island and the row say so, with a retry, and the queue never skips by itself.
8. The player knows no provider. `playableSource` in `player/model.ts` is the only reader of the catalog's audio field, and lengths and positions come from the engine, not from the catalog.
9. Expanded is derived, never stored. There is one route, `/track/:id`: the screen is the expanded player when the route's track is the player's current track and the screen is a compact one (`isPlayerExpanded` in `model.ts`), and the plain track page with a Play control and the island below otherwise. After a reload nothing is current, so a track's URL is its plain page.
10. Opening the player is a view transition, and so is closing it with its handle or the browser's Back; on a wide screen the two are one transition in two directions. On a narrow screen closing is its own: the sheet goes down off the foot of the screen as one piece, the way it is swiped away, and the island comes in where it stands (`sheet-goes-down` in `player/transitions.css`). The sheet drawing back into its island came apart on a phone into one thing after another. A sheet swiped away is never a transition, and nothing is done as the listener lets go of it: it goes on by the browser's own scroll, and holding it where it stood made it jump back to the top first. What follows is the opening, and the closing on a wide screen: the island and the expanded player share the name `player`, so the capsule grows into the screen and shrinks back, and the cover's name is on whichever of them is drawn. A track change inside the expanded player is no transition, and neither is a player whose sheet is already off the screen: the page behind it is what the listener sees (`viewTransition` reads the sheet's `reach`). A sheet let go of part of the way down is a transition like the others, from where the sheet is. The island's own picture does not travel with the box that grows into the sheet: it is kept at the foot of that box, where the island stands, gone early on the way out and back late on the way in, so it is never seen to rise up the screen or to come down from a place it never stood. Both pictures carry the one name, so the app names the two transitions (`player-opens` and `player-closes`, from `viewTransition` in main.ts) and the style sheet knows by them which picture is the island's. The expanded player has no blur: its picture is drawn again on every frame of a view transition, and a blur at a new size each time costs more than a frame has, which is what once made the island open in jumps.

    The expanded player started from the design (`design/album.pen`, "Isla abierta"; its motion in `design/motion/isla`) and has three faces (`pane` in the player's Model). On the song's, the cover fills the head of the sheet from edge to edge under a veil, with the track's title over its foot and nothing else over it; under it are the progress, with the time left counted down, the three controls, the main one 84 across, and under those two more: three dots at the near end, which open a menu (`player/moreMenu.ts`), and the way to the queue at the far one. The menu offers Go to album, which is followed as a link to the album's page would be (the player is held until the page can take the screen, and then shrinks to its island over it), and Song details, which turns the player to the face that says what the catalog knows of the track. Nothing more is laid out under the controls, so the song's face needs no scrolling. On the queue's face the cover is a thumbnail beside the title, in a low head right under the handle, and the songs to come are listed by their place in all that is played, each row ending in a handle where a list's row has the song's length. A song is taken up by its handle and carried to another place in the queue, or moved there with the keys (Foldkit UI's `DragAndDrop`, its Model the queue's own, `drag` in `player/queue/`): the row it came from grows dim, the song is drawn at the height of the finger by the shell (`queueGhostView`, since the sheet is a box that what is in it is placed by), going up and down only and standing across where the list stands, since the finger's drift to either side says nothing in a list that runs one way, and a line shows where it would be put down. The rows do not move to show the place, which would move what the place is measured by. That is all that is done to the queue's order, with nothing to switch on first; the face of the song's details has the same head over what the catalog knows. The head is kept low and bare, with no name of the face over it, so that the room is for what the face lists. On both, the deck, the progress, the three controls and the two under them, stands at the foot of the sheet over what scrolls, the head itself leads back to the song, and the way to the queue, lit while the queue is open, leads to it and back. No face has a control of its own to close it. The deck is one piece on every face (`DECKS` in `player/view.ts` says where it stands), and what is in the sheet is put back at its top when the face changes (`ShowFaceFromTop`).

    The handle is the sheet's and no face's: a short bar at the very top of the sheet, as on any sheet that is swiped away, which stands there whatever face is shown and however far what is in the sheet has been scrolled. It says how the player is closed and, pressed, closes it. There is no other control for that. Nothing in the sheet, nor the bar an album's head draws back to, adds the inset of the screen's top: the pages are drawn under it already, and a browser that draws its own bar over the page (Brave on an iPhone) reports that inset to a page that is not under it, which once put the handle far down the sheet and made the album's bar twice its height.

    The cover travels: from the island's disc to the head of the sheet as the player opens (380 ms), and between the head and the thumbnail as it turns to the queue and back (280 ms, `player-queue-opens` and `player-queue-closes`), when the deck travels too. In the sheet the cover lies under the veil and the title, and a bare cover that simply arrived would lie over the title until the transition ended. So the song's face has the cover twice: the one that carries its name in a view transition, and over it one with no name, which stays in the sheet's picture. The one that travels lands on the other and fades into it. Until it has landed, in the last fifth of the time, the head of the sheet shows nothing of its own, neither cover nor veil nor title (`head-comes-last`): the cover that travels grows from the island's disc and the sheet's box from the whole island, so on the way the cover is narrower than the sheet, and a cover the sheet already showed at its full size was seen beside it, the same picture twice and a step apart. The box that grows from the island is the sheet's colour from the start, and what is in the sheet comes on it once the box is large enough to read. The times of all of it are in `player/transitions.css`, worked out from the design's own account of the motion. Closing is not opening the other way round: the way is eased and goes fast at its start whichever way it is travelled, so on the way back the cover is small by the time a third of the time has passed. A cover that came over the sheet's own little by little was then a small picture seen over the same picture large, and the player was seen to close twice. So the cover takes over at the very start, while the two are one size, the sheet's face is gone by a quarter of the way, and the island comes only when the cover is nearly on its disc. Closed from the queue's face or the song's details, where the cover is a thumbnail with nothing under it, the thumbnail is there from the first frame and travels as it is (`player-face-closes`); and the face goes with the player, so opened again it shows the song.

    The expanded player is drawn on a sheet (`packages/web/src/sheet/`, a Foldkit Submodel that follows React Aria's Sheet): the listener swipes it down to close it. The swipe is the browser's own scroll, not the app following a finger: the sheet stands on a stage inside a box that scrolls and snaps to the two places where the sheet is whole and where it is gone, so it has the momentum and the stretch of any scroll on the device (`sheet/geometry.ts`). What is in the sheet scrolls only while the sheet is whole, and from its top a swipe down goes on to the sheet. The Mount `FollowSheet` puts the box where the sheet is whole as it enters the document and tells the Model how much of the sheet is on screen (`reach`: `Whole`, `Partly`, `Gone`). The sheet also remembers a keyboard dismissal across the history callback. While the sheet is short of whole, the page the player was opened over is drawn behind it as it was left, with its navigation and its island, out of reach and with no names in a view transition, and a backdrop fades with the swipe by the sheet's own timeline (`sheet-backdrop` in `sheet/sheet.css`). Let go of on its way down, far enough along or fast enough to be going, the sheet says `Leaving` while part of it is still on screen (`watchLettingGo` in `sheet/mount.ts` tells it, at the moment the finger lifts, and holds the sheet where it stands), and the player closes from there as its handle closes it: it is seen to draw back into its island from wherever it was let go, and does not slide on off the foot of the screen past an island it would then have nothing to do with. Should it get off the screen all the same, moved by something other than a finger, the sheet says `Left` and the player closes as its control closes it, back through the history, with nothing more to see; with no page behind, Home takes the screen at once. Escape says `RequestedClose` and restores the previous page immediately, or Home on direct entry.

11. The island is one figure, light on the light page and dark on the dark one, with one outline round three things: the cover, a disc at its left end that stands out a little above and below the body; the body, which curves to meet it; and a swell at the other end that holds the terracotta control that starts and stops the sound. Past the swell a lower tail holds Next while there is room for it; in the row with the navigation's circle and Search the tail is drawn in and the swell is the island's end. Going there is three movements, each setting off after the one before so that each can be followed: Next travels left with the end of the tail and in under the main control, which stays where it is; the island narrows; and it comes down into the row. Narrowing and coming down are one movement on a spring, and so is everything else that moves there: each piece goes a little past its place and comes back to it, which is what makes the whole read as one soft movement. The springs were read off the app they follow, frame by frame, from where the island's main control stood in each: coming out, the island goes 4% past its place in a fifth of a second and is at rest in under half of one; drawing back, 2.5% past in three tenths. The menu and Search are stiffer, 1.5% and half of one percent, since a circle that overshot would be seen to lose its shape. They are the `--ease-island-` and `--ease-menu-` curves in `view/dock.css`, written out as `linear()` because CSS has no spring. Each curve is a spring written out over its whole time, half a second coming out and a little over six tenths drawing back (`--dock-out`, `--dock-back`), and is played at that time: given less, it is the same spring played fast, past its place and back before the eye has seen it give, which is how the dock once came to move with no bounce at all. The island's width is the layout's and changes in one frame, so what is drawn of it is set back to the width it had and brought to the new one by script (`dockResizeMotion` in `player/island/mount.ts`), and that goes by the very clock its place goes by: the same time, curve and wait, read from the style sheet. Where the island's far end is seen is where it stands plus how wide it is drawn; when the width got there in a fraction of the spring's time, coming out threw that end, and the light on it, past the edge of the screen, and drawing back let it fall short and go on, which read as a great bounce. Coming out is the same three the other way round. The figure takes the cover's colours near the cover, as bright as they are. The island's words are one colour throughout, dark on the light island and light on the dark one, whatever the cover. Its edge is its own colour: at rest there is no border to see, and a soft shadow tells it from the page. What travels that edge is a light, not a line: a short copper beam with an almost white head, and a soft glow round it that spills to both sides of the edge. Only the engine's word that there is sound sets it going, at a slow and steady pace. It never starts or stops at once: put to rest (a pause, a stop, a failure, sound that ran out of data) it slows to a halt where it is and dims to an ember that stays there, and set going again it brightens and gathers speed from that same place. A tap not yet answered changes nothing, however long the start takes, so going on to another track does not halt it and a first track does not light it. Until a track is played there is no island, and the first one brings it: it is seen to come, rising into its place and growing clear on the way, from under the foot of the screen where it stands there and a short way where it stands over the navigation. `viewTransition` says `island-appears` with `choose-track` for that track and no other, and the style sheet moves the island's box, its shadow and its light by one clock, the cover's own on its way from its row, so the cover lands on its disc as the island comes to rest. From a list with no picture of the track (an album's rows) nothing travels, and the island rises with its cover on it. Previous and the progress bar are the expanded player's; on a desktop's screen, where there is none, both stand in the island (13). Wherever it stands, Previous is not to be pressed while no track was played before the current one, as Next is not after the last: the first track has only itself behind it. A headset's or a lock screen's Previous still takes that track back to its start. The main control is one colour in both, the island's (`--color-island-control`), and the three glyphs of the transport are the design's. What a control alone cannot explain (stopped by the system, failed) stands in a few words where the artist was, and is said whole to assistive technology. A title too long for the island is a marquee: it waits, travels across once with its copy following it in, and waits again. So is who the track is by, under it: each of the two lines is a marquee of its own, with its own waits and passes (`title` and `artist` in the island's Model, each a `marquee/`), so either may travel while the other stands. The island changing width under it disturbs nothing, since a pass goes as far as the title is long and not as its place is wide: the wait or the pass under way goes on, and a title that comes to fit in the middle of a pass ends that pass before it stands (`Finishing`). All of this is in the architecture, with nothing of its own outside it. What the light does (`light`) and where each line of words is in its waits and passes (`title`, `artist`) are in the island's Model (`player/island/`, a Submodel the player tells what the sound is doing and when its track changes), the waits and the passes are Commands, and the view draws the figure, the light and the title from them. What only the document knows reaches it through Foldkit's `Mount`, an effect bound to one element: a marquee's `Measure` (`marquee/mount.ts`) tells the Model whether its line fits and how far a pass takes it, for the title and for the line under it alike; the island's `DrawFigure` (`player/island/mount.ts`) writes the island's outline, worked out in `view/islandOutline.ts`, to the figure's paths in the very frame the island changes width, since a width sent through the Model would be drawn a frame late, and in that same frame puts the light back where the listener saw it, because the light is placed in shares of the outline and an outline that grows or shrinks would carry it along the edge; `StartLight` sets the light's animations to what the Model says as the island enters the document. From then on `update` gives the Command `ChangeLightPace` whenever the Model's `light` changes, and the light's pace changes little by little; a change that overtakes one under way carries on from the pace the light had reached. While a song changes, the island is one side of a view transition whose box is cut with `clip-path` drawn a little outside it, because the cover and the swell stand out of the island's box. The light and the shadow have view transition names of their own (`island-light` and `island-shadow`, said where each is drawn, in `player/island/figure.ts`; `player/island.css` draws their pictures): a transition fades a still picture of the island into the live one, and a light inside that picture would stand still for as long as the fade lasts, as a shadow inside it would be cut where the picture is cut.

12. A row is one surface and ends in one control, three dots, that opens a menu of what else can be done with its track. A press on the row lights all of it, the control's place included, and a lit surface stands free: the line under its row and the line under the row above are not drawn, since a straight line would run into its rounded corners (`rowClass` in `view/trackRow.ts`). The menu offers, in this order: Play next, which puts the track right after the current one; Add to queue, which puts it after all the rest; Go to album, which is followed as a link to the page of the album the track is on would be; and Song details, which is followed as a link to the track's page would be (navigation rule 1). Go to album is offered on every row but those of the album's own page, for as long as the catalog has the album. A track is queued only once something is playing and is never the current track, the queue holds a track once among those to come, and queueing changes nothing that sounds. What was chosen is answered where it was chosen. Choosing a page, the song's or the album's, leaves the menu open: the item that was pressed turns its mark while the page is on its way, and if the page fails to open the item says so and a second press of it tries again, so nobody opens the menu twice to retry. The menu goes with the list when the page takes the screen. With the menu closed, the row's control turns instead, and a failure is a mark in that control's place (navigation rule 6): nothing is said under the row. A track with nothing to play says "No audio" where its length would be, and one that could not be played shows a mark where it would have shown that it sounds; both are said in words to assistive technology only. Queueing closes the menu, and the control shows a tick for a moment, said in words to assistive technology only. What the catalog said of a track is kept under the track's id, so every row for that track reads it. An album is on many rows, so what the catalog said of it does not tell which row was used: the Model notes the row each album was last asked from (`albumRows`), and that row alone turns its control, or marks in it that the album could not be opened or is not available. An album chosen from a shelf has no such row, and its tile turns instead. The menu is Foldkit UI's `Menu`, one Model for each row kept under the row's id (`menus` in `rows/`, a Submodel of the app's; a menu never touched is not kept), and `rows/model.ts` says what it can offer. The rows tell the app what was chosen from a row's menu and do nothing about it themselves. It is not modal: its backdrop covers the screen and hears the press that closes it. The menu under the expanded player's controls is closed the same way, by a press anywhere else, the control that opened it among that: its backdrop is drawn over the player's sheet, which stands at the height a row's backdrop has, and on a compact screen it is closed for good once the player is its island again. On a desktop's screen the menu is the island's own (13).

13. On a desktop's screen, 64rem and wider, the player does not expand: there is room for three columns, the navigation, the page and the queue (`design/desktop.pen`). Whether the screen is one is in the Model (`screen`, `Compact` or `Desktop`, in `screen.ts`), told by the Subscription `screens` as the app starts and whenever the window crosses that width. It is the one width the Model holds, because it changes what the player is and not only where its pieces are drawn. The navigation going to the side at 48rem is still the style sheet's alone, and between the two widths the player is a sheet.

    There the island's words are no link, and it has of its own what the sheet would have held: the progress of the sound, the way to the queue and the three dots of the player's menu before its transport, and Previous in the transport. The menu offers Go to album and Song details. Song details is followed as a link to the track's page would be: on this screen that is always the plain page, waited for like any other, and with no row to state a failure on, it takes the screen and says it.

    The progress of the sound is on the island too, in a chin under its body (`design/desktop.pen`): the island's figure has a second, lower piece there, from past the cover to just before the swell, with a round corner at each end that curves up into the disc above it (`chinUnderside` in `view/islandOutline.ts`, drawn when the island says `data-island-chin`). In it are the time played, the bar, and the time left counted down. It is the expanded player's own slider (`seekSlider`), and is dragged, pressed or moved with the keys as that one is. The island's box is as high as the body and the chin together, so a picture of it taken for a view transition has all of it; the light travels the chin's edge with the rest.

    The queue opens in a column of its own past the page (`column` in the queue's Model, `player/queue/`; it is the queue's and no session's, so it stays open across pages and across lists). At its head are its name and the control that closes it, which hands the focus back to the island's; under them the song that plays next, on a card with its cover, and those that play later, each with its place, who it is by, its length and its handle. It is the same queue, moved the same way: the card and the list are one place to put a song down in. It is no route. Opening and closing it moves neither the URL nor the history, and the page beside it is the page on screen, in reach. The column, the page and the navigation each scroll by themselves.

    The column is between 20 and 26.5rem wide, by the screen's width, and the navigation is 11rem there, so the page never has less room than it has beside the navigation at 48rem. A page whose layout depends on its width goes by its own (`@container` on a track's page), not the screen's. The column comes in from its side and goes back off it as a view transition (`queue-column-opens` and `queue-column-closes`): the page has its new width in the one frame, since a width that changed little by little would lay every shelf out again on every frame, and the island goes to its new place with the column. Crossing the width with the window moves nothing in the history and is no transition: the page of the track the player holds is the sheet on one side of it and that track's plain page on the other.

14. The system's own controls for a player, a lock screen's, a headset's or a keyboard's, do what the player's do (`mediaKeys` in `player/subscription.ts`, the Media Session API): play, pause, next, previous, and its bar for the sound, which moved says where to (`seekto`) and asks the engine for that place as a key on the player's own bar does. Each control is handed over by itself, since a browser refuses one it does not know by throwing. What is playing is told to the system (`PublishNowPlaying`); where the sound is, how long it is and whether it sounds are not: with nothing said a browser reads them off the audio element, which is the engine's and the only thing that knows.

## How it looks and installs

1. The palette is aubergine, built on the two colours of the mark: the deep aubergine of the speaker (`#46203f`) and the pale orchid it stands on (`#efdfec`). Every colour is a token in `styles.css`, light and dark, and no class names a colour of its own: what stands over a picture, the same on the light page and the dark one, is a token too (`--color-night`, `--color-on-night`, `--color-shade`).
2. A shelf snaps: a swipe along it comes to rest with a tile at its leading edge. Where there is a pointer (`hover: hover` and `pointer: fine`, whatever the screen's width) a row that goes on sideways, a shelf or a list in columns (10), has no scrollbar and is turned by two arrows, one at each end, that come when the pointer is over the row (`view/turning.ts`; `turning` in `view/turning.css`). Each turns it a screenful and never skips a piece: onward, the first piece not seen whole comes to the leading edge (`turnedFrom` in `turn.ts`, `TurnRow` in `command.ts`). Where the row stands is the document's and not the Model's, so that an arrow is gone when there is no more its way is read off the row's own scroll by the style sheet (a scroll timeline), and a row that shows all it holds has none. The arrows are out of the order of focus, since the focus going from one piece to the next turns the row by itself. A trackpad still moves the row. On a screen that is touched there are no arrows and the row is swiped, with its scrollbar as it was.
3. An album's cover travels between its tile on a shelf and the head of its page, as a track's does between its row and its page: the tile's picture and the page's carry one name on the page on screen (`pictureFlightStyle`, by the tile's kind). On the page the name is on the whole head, the cover with the title and the facts over it (`albumHeadFlightAttributes`), so the words grow out of the tile with the cover and shrink back into it with it; a cover that travelled alone would land bare, and the words would appear over it a step behind. An artist's picture, a playlist's, a genre's and a station's travel the same way: what a kind's picture travels as is one table (`FLIGHTS` in `view/artwork.ts`).
4. A change of page is seen to happen, and how says what kind of change it was (`viewTransition` in `main.ts` names it, and `view/pageTransitions.css` draws it). From one section to another is a change of place: the page gives way where it stands and the other comes in its place, the two never seen together. The navigation's mark does not wait for that: it travels from the item the listener left to the one they chose as they choose it (`maybeMarkedDestination` in `view.ts`; `dock-mark`, one piece for the whole menu and not each item's own ground), and the chosen item's own icon breathes there for as long as its page takes (`BREATHES_CLASS` in `view/icon.ts`), with no spinner in its place. A mark that moved only once the page had come would read as the app being slow to hear. Which page is on screen is still said by `aria-current`, which moves when the page does. Opening a page from a list is a step deeper: the picture the two share travels between them (3) and, where the list has no picture of it to carry (a song's page from a row's menu on an album, which has no thumbnails), the page itself comes over the list from the far side while the list steps back under it, and goes back off it the same way. Which of the two it is only the document knows, so `entry.ts` decides it, and notes which pages came over so that they go back as they came. A move through the history that the browser drew itself (the swipe from the screen's edge that goes back or forward) is not drawn a second time, and nothing else is set moving for it either: the browser says so on its `popstate`, `entry.ts` says it on the document until the app has drawn the page that answers the move, and for that drawing there is no view transition, every CSS transition is held, and what moves by script stands still (`view/shownByBrowser.ts`). It is held by that drawing and not by a clock: after such a swipe Safari keeps the page from drawing for a third of a second or so.
5. An album's head draws back to a bar as its page is scrolled, by the scroll and not by a clock. The cover is never seen to shrink: for all of the way but the very last it keeps its size, goes up with the page and only grows dim, until it is clear; then, as the bar is about to arrive, the bar's own thumbnail grows bright in the bar, where it stands, with the bar's title: nothing of the cover moves to get there. A thumbnail of the head's, put where the bar would be and taken over by the bar's as it arrived, went on up past the bar's place for a frame or two on a phone, and was seen to hit the top of the screen and drop back. A cover that grew smaller while it could still be seen read as a picture being squeezed, however late it began and however dim it was. The words go up with the page and grow smaller towards the place the bar's title has, where they give way to it, so the title is seen to rise and settle in the bar. There is one cover to see wherever the page is scrolled. Every album's songs are at least as tall as what the screen has left under the bar and over the room at the page's foot (`--page-foot`, the navigation's and the island's, in `view/layout.ts`), however few they are, so that any album can be scrolled far enough for its head to finish: a page too short for that was left with its cover on its way for good. That is as far as a short album scrolls, and no further: its first song comes to rest right under the bar. With the foot's room left in, an album was scrolled that much past its head's end, and one of a single song went up under the bar and out of sight. The bar is as high as a bar of the system's (`--spacing-album-bar` in `styles.css`) and takes over only once the cover has landed.
6. The mark of the track the player holds, on its row, holds its spinner and its bars both, one over the other, and the one not shown is small and clear: a start slow enough to turn the spinner becomes sound where it stands (`MARK_PIECES` in `view/trackRow.ts`). The queue's rows are a list's rows, with the same surface that a press lights (`rowSurfaceClass`).
7. A tile whose page is on its way says so with its own picture, and nothing is laid over it: the picture is a sleeve, which moves a little aside, and its record comes part of the way out from behind it and turns for as long as the page takes (`recordView` and `sleeveClass` in `view/shelf.ts`; the alternatives it was chosen from are in `design/motion/tile-loaders.html`). It is clear, not hidden, while it is behind its sleeve: a page kept behind the one on screen is hidden as a whole, and a piece of it that said it was visible would be seen through it. The record is no part of the piece that travels to the page's head: the picture goes, and the record stays with the shelf. Under reduced motion it is out and still. When the page could not be asked (navigation rule 6) the record breaks, as `design/motion/vinyl-errors` draws it ("Corte"): it turns whole while the page is on its way, and only then a scratch runs across it from its edge to the sleeve, in 400 ms; as it reaches the sleeve, at 210 ms, the record comes apart in two along it and the sleeve takes the knock, each in 190 ms, and its light stops where it was. The record is two parts cut along that line, the far one there and not seen while it is whole, so that its light turns with the record's; each part carries where it is cut and where it goes (`--record-cut`, `--record-apart`), and `view/record.css` is the way there. A row's thumbnail has a record only to break: it comes out when the row's page could not be asked, and breaks once it is out (`--record-breaks-after`). Under reduced motion the record is broken at once.
8. Bafle installs to a home screen. The manifest, the icons and the mark are in `packages/web/public` (their sources in `design/`). A link to it shared elsewhere (X, Slack, a message) is shown as one large card for every page, "Music in motion." beside an album's page and the player on the deep aubergine (`og-image.png`, exported from `design/og-card.pen`; the `og:` tags in `index.html`, whose `?v=` changes with the picture so that a card already read is read again), since what reads a card runs no script. A home screen is given one picture, `apple-touch-icon.png`, drawn at 512 pixels from `design/apple-touch-icon.svg`: larger than any home screen draws it, so it is only ever brought down to size. It is the mark with its two colours the other way round, the pale orchid speaker on the deep aubergine, because iOS from 26 keeps an icon's ground as it is and fades what stands on it towards that ground, down the icon: the dark speaker on the pale ground came out washed, and the pale one on the dark ground comes out shaded, as a thing lit from above (seen side by side in the Simulator). While an iPhone starts the installed app it shows a launch picture of the screen's exact size, light or dark (`public/splash`, drawn from the mark); while the app itself loads, in any browser, the same mark stands in the page (`#splash` in `index.html`) and gives way when the first page has something of its own to show: what the catalog said of it, with the pictures it opens with (navigation rule 1), or that it could not be asked. The app's first drawing is only placeholders, and the mark stands over them: the shell says `data-launching` for as long as the Model's `launch` is `Launching`, and the mark reads it. The bar a phone draws of its own over the installed app is the colour the document says (`theme-color` in `index.html`, one for each colour scheme), and that is always the ground of what is under it, so the two are one surface: the mark's ground while the mark stands, and from the launch on the ground of the app's pages, light or dark, which each `theme-color` carries and takes then (`PaintSystemBar`, given by `launchOnceShown`). Once the mark has given way it is not in the page at all (`display: none`), since Safari from 26 reads the colour of its own bars off what lies along the top of the page, a clear piece among that. Launched is for good, so a page that waits after that, a first search among them, waits in the app. The app needs the network to play anything, and nothing of the catalog or the sound is kept. What is kept is the app's own shell, by a service worker (`public/sw.js`, registered in builds only): the page it opens with and the files that page names. So the installed app starts with no network, on its mark, and says in its own words that it could not reach the catalog, with the way to try again.
9. No page has a control of its own to go back, nor one that leads Home. The navigation is in reach from every page (navigation rule 11) and leads to any section, the island leads to the player, and the page behind is the browser's: its Back, or the swipe from the screen's edge, finds that page as it was left (navigation rule 9). A control at the page's corner said nothing those do not, and stood over the head of every page that is no section. So a page that says a failure offers "Try again" and nothing else, and one that says something is not available offers nothing. The one control that goes back through the history is the handle of the expanded player's sheet, since under the player there is no navigation.
10. Top songs look one way wherever they are listed, on New, on an artist's page and on a genre's. Where the list is wide enough for two columns of rows (42rem) they are never taller than five rows, however many they are: they take the page's width (`TrackSpread` in `view/trackList.ts`) and are laid out in columns of five at most (`ROWS_IN_COLUMN`), as even as they can be, the first rows down the first column and the next down the one after, so the chart still reads from its top. The columns stand in a row that goes on sideways and snaps, a column at its leading edge, as a shelf does (2): two are seen, and when there are more the next one shows at the edge (`COLUMNS_CLASS` in `view/trackList.ts`). So what stands under a long chart is in reach. Narrower than that, on a phone, the list is one column of all its rows, as an album's is: a row that went on sideways there showed one column at a time and hid the rest of the chart behind a swipe. Which of the two goes by the list's own width, not the screen's, since the queue opens beside the page and takes room from it. The placeholders stand in those same columns. The songs a search found past the first four are laid out the same way, under a heading of their own, "More songs", at any width. The songs of an album, a playlist and a station are one column of a width that reads well, as long as the list is: they are what the page is.
11. That the device has no connection, or that a page shows what it loaded earlier because it could not be refreshed, is said over the page and not in it: a notice at the top of the screen that takes no room (`noticeView`, `noticeOf` in `view.ts`), with "Try again" while there is a connection to try with, and a control that puts it away (`staleNotice` in the Model). The browser's own word that the connection is lost brings it at once, before anything has been asked of the catalog (`LostConnection`, from `connectionChanges` in `subscription.ts`; `connection` in the Model): "You are offline", and "Showing what loaded earlier" when the page holds something. A page that has nothing to show but that it could not be asked says so itself, with no notice over it. The notice comes down from the top as it is needed and goes back up as soon as it is not, saying what it said as it goes (`lastNoticeReason`): it is always in the document, and gone, nothing in it can be reached or heard. It stands at the top as the listener asked, over whatever a page keeps there as it scrolls, Search's field and the bar an album's head draws back to, and on a phone over the page's title: it is put away by its own control. The page under it does not move when the notice comes or goes. Put away, it comes back the next time the catalog cannot be asked, or the connection is lost. When the browser says the connection is back (`RegainedConnection`) the notice goes, what could not be had for want of it is forgotten, so that a row's menu and a tile that said so are at rest again (`forgetOfflineFailures` in `main.ts`), and the page on screen that could not be reached is asked again by itself, so nothing has to be pressed; the focus is not moved for it, and a page the listener chose and is waiting for is still waited for.
12. A track's own page offers Play next and Add to queue beside its Play control, once something else is playing: what a row's menu offers for its track.
13. Search has two layouts, told apart by the page's own width and not the screen's, since the queue opens beside the page (`FIELD_PLACES` and the `search-wide:` variant in `page/search.ts`; the designs are `design/motion/search` and `design/desktop.pen`). Narrow, it opens on the mark and its name over the field, in the middle of the screen. The field stays there while nothing is asked and while the first answer is on its way, when its magnifier breathes as a destination's icon does: that is the shape Search opens with, and it has no other placeholders. Once the catalog has answered, with what it found, with nothing or with a failure, the field goes up to the head of the page and the answer comes in under it; it stays there, over the page that scrolls under it, until it is emptied, by its own control or by Escape, and then comes back down. Where the field stands is derived, never stored (`hasSearchAnswer` in `model.ts`), and the page says it in `data-field`. The field travels for 400 ms, a little longer than the design's 280, which was too quick to follow on a phone; the other times keep the design's shares of it (`--search-travel`): the mark and the name are gone in its first third, and what was found comes once it is half way up. A phone moves the whole page up to keep a field being typed in clear of its keyboard, so a field that then goes up to the page's head would go off the top of what is seen: as it goes up, with Search on screen, the screen is brought back to its top with it (`ShowSearchFromTop`, given by `update` whenever the field has just gone to the head). A finger that drags the page, anywhere but on the field's own box, takes the focus from the field, so a phone puts its keyboard away and what was found is seen whole (`dragsUnderSearchField` in `subscription.ts`): it is the drag that is heard, not the scroll, which the app also makes itself. Wide, it opens the same way, on the mark and the name over the field in the middle; with an answer the name heads the page as every section's does and the field stands at the far end of that row, smaller and squarer, as the design draws it. The field goes from the one place and size to the other in the same 400 ms: it is placed by the page and not laid out in it, so nothing else is laid out again on the way. The name is one piece in two places and cannot travel between them, so it goes at once and comes where it now stands as the field arrives. What was found is the same in both: the artist found first on a card of their own, which is their tile to the app: its picture travels to their page, the focus is given back to it, and while their page is on its way the card is a sleeve as a tile's picture is, moving a little aside as its record comes part of the way out past its far end and turns. The artist's large portrait fills the card, with the name in white over the same bottom gradient as an album cover. Search shows the first four songs beside the card on a wide page, and the rest under the albums as "More songs", which on a narrow page stand right under the first four and read as one list; the albums, three across in a row that snaps on a narrow page and a grid that fills the width on a wide one; then the other artists and the playlists on shelves.
